## Readings and translation decisions

- **31–32:** The sentence begins in 30b and ends in 32a. The three are Rāma, Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa. Neither Bharadvāja nor Guha is silently added to that trio. The translation of 31 remains a participial fragment. It does not borrow the following finite verb. The parallel's apparent रंयम् is not imported over the source's रम्यम्.
- **32–33:** The verse boundary divides the residence in the forest from Daśaratha's grief, and divides his grief from his named final action. English and Hindi add `[Daśaratha]` / `[दशरथ]` only as explicitly bracketed subjects. “Went to heaven” preserves the narrative's statement rather than replacing it with a modern medical claim.
- **33:** The imported मृते is retained; the parallel reads गते. The indexed IIT base Sanskrit corroborates मृते. `द्विजैः` is rendered “Brahmins” in this Vasiṣṭha-led context in English and retained as द्विज in Hindi. No modern rank or universal entitlement is inferred.
- **33–34:** The relative clause and Hindi instrumental fragment continue across the numbered boundary. The appointment/refusal is not moved wholesale into verse 33.
- **34:** रामपादप्रसादकः is understood as seeking Rāma's favour, with the feet conveying reverence. It is not confused with the transfer of the sandals in 37.
- **35–36:** The imported सुमहात्मानं, also visible in the indexed IIT base text, is preserved. The parallel has स महात्मानम्. Bharata's direct words belong to 36a. “You alone are king” remains Bharata's assertion within this succession episode.
- **36–37:** Rāma's descriptors in 36b continue to his refusal in 37a. No extra action is supplied to make verse 36 stand alone.
- **37–38:** The imported दत्वा is retained and romanized `datvā`; the parallel writes दत्त्वा. This batch does not silently normalize that spelling or claim exact lexical identity between witnesses. न्यासम् is a trust/deposit. पुनःपुनः is linked with persuading Bharata to return in 38, not with repeated gifts of sandals.
- **38–39:** रामपादौ is translated literally as Rāma's feet. Some reference explanations associate it with the sandals, but that interpretation is not substituted for the source word. Bharata's rule at Nandigrāma remains in 39a.
- **39–40:** The subject changes from Bharata to Rāma within 39. Hindi supplies `[राम]` transparently. आलक्ष्य is rendered as anticipating further visits, consistent with the narrative connection, without claiming that repeated visits had already occurred.
- The narrator remains Nārada addressing Vālmīki. Bharata's embedded speech in 36 is marked as speech. Rāma's refusal is narrated, not turned into an invented quotation.
- Modern context and selected teachings are bilingual editorial reflections, displayed in their own sections. They are not additions to the Sanskrit or universal commands inferred from a royal family's choices.
