## Translation and interpretation decisions

Nārada narrates to Vālmīki throughout. No direct speech is invented. The first half of 61 completes 60b, with Sugrīva's understood name bracketed. The second half begins the passive clause completed in 62a. Verse 62b states Rāma's pledge to slay Vālin; 63a gives Sugrīva's account of Vālin's strength. The doubt in 63b leads into 64. These links remain bilingual connected-verse notes, separate from translation, modern context and selected grounded teachings.

In 64, supplied “strength” and “him” are bracketed. राघवप्रत्ययार्थं is understood as seeking assurance about Rāghava, in the context of Sugrīva's doubt. The parallel's printed separated form is disclosed. The body is described here; bones occur in 65. No account of Dundubhi's death, earlier throwing distance, or additional dialogue is inserted. Verse 65 keeps ten yojanas, without a fixed kilometre or mile conversion. Its understood subject and object are marked in brackets.

Verse 66 names sāla trees, not palmyra trees. Rasātala remains the epic's named lower world, without a modern geological identification. The extraordinary action is translated as narrative, without a claim of physical measurement. The imported तथा remains unchanged despite the distinct indexed IIT तदा witness. Verse 67 links Sugrīva's pleasure and confidence to that deed; गुहाम् remains “cave”, without establishing a modern site.

In 68, the vānara lord is identified as [Vālin]. In 69a that same subject persuades Tārā and meets Sugrīva; in 69b the subject changes to Rāma. Supplied names and the understood combat setting are bracketed. No words of Tārā's counsel, Vālin's response, firing position or ethical justification are imported from the longer episode or commentary. Verse 70 refers back to that killing, not a second one, and establishes Sugrīva in the kingdom. The understood office is bracketed. No coronation ceremony is invented. No general teaching endorsing violence for a friend is inferred.

English and Hindi were independently composed after the boundary audit and editorially reviewed by the assistant. No independent human reviewer is claimed. Modern reflections at 61, 63 and 66 concern shared commitments, checking ability and relevant evidence. They do not claim to be additional Sanskrit commands.
