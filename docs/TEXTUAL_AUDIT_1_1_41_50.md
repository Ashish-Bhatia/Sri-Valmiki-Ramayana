## Readings and translation decisions

- **41–42:** Rāma's seeing of the sages continues across the boundary. Verse 41 names only Śarabhaṅga. The remaining names stay in 42a. Agastya's brother is not given an inferred personal name. राजीवलोचनः retains its lotus-eye comparison.
- **42–43:** ऐन्द्रम् is rendered as Indra's bow. No genealogy of the weapon is added. The accepted objects continue into 43a, where तूणी…अक्षयसायकौ supplies two quivers with inexhaustible arrows. Bracketed words make the carried-over verb transparent. This epic description is not converted into a modern technical claim.
- **43–44:** वसतस्तस्य रामस्य begins a residence clause completed by the sages' arrival in 44a. वनचरैस्सह stays attached to Rāma's forest residence. “Forest dwellers” does not silently narrow the word to a named community or add unmentioned people.
- **44–45:** असुररक्षसाम् is translated as asuras and rākṣasas. प्रतिशुश्राव is a promise or assent, not merely hearing. The explicit promise of killing in combat remains in 45. अग्निकल्पानाम् retains the comparison to fire. No speech in quotation marks is invented.
- **46:** तेन refers back to Rāma in this condensed account. The literal attribution is retained; this verse gives no details of the injuries or sequence of actions. The longer episode must be read for those details. No teaching endorsing disfigurement or punishment for rejection is attached to this verse.
- **47–48:** Verse 47 is an accusative list; its finite verb निजघान occurs in 48a. The translation retains that connection rather than moving the verb into 47. Neither fourteen initial attackers nor a two-stage battle account is imported from commentary. The raw **वने** (“in the forest”) is preserved and translated in 48a; the complete parallel has **रणे** (“in battle”). Its possible status as a transcription error is unresolved, so this boundary-only overlay does not silently emend it. Internal sandhi spellings such as ततश्शूर्पणखा… are also retained.
- **48–49:** The residence and Janasthāna phrase begins a new sentence in 48b. रक्षसाम् and the count of fourteen thousand remain in 49a. Brackets mark supplied subjects. The count is presented as part of the narrative, without claiming independent historical verification.
- **49–50:** ज्ञाति means kin; “cousins” is not substituted. Rāvaṇa's anger is not used to invent an unspoken motive or decision. His selection of Mārīca as an ally belongs to 50a. Mārīca's dissuasion in 50b remains a continuing clause. The warning's words in 51a and Rāvaṇa's subsequent action are not prematurely translated here.
- Nārada continues narrating to Vālmīki throughout this batch. The sages' request and Rāma's promise are narrated. Mārīca is named as the one dissuading Rāvaṇa, but his quoted words fall in the next batch.
- Connected-verse notes, modern context and selected teachings are bilingual fields displayed separately from the translations. Teachings are editorial inferences, not new Sanskrit or universal commands.
