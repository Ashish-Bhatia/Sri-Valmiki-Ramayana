THE TEXT AND ITS TRANSMISSION

Sanskrit source & editions

Seven kāṇḍas, followed in their traditional order. The Sanskrit, translations and reflections remain distinct.

Which Sanskrit text?

The Rāmāyaṇa survives in several manuscript traditions and published editions. No surviving manuscript is Vālmīki’s own handwritten original. This reader uses an attributed digital Sanskrit transcription, with corrections shown alongside its imported reading.

The underlying text was obtained from the Valmiki Ramayan Dataset. Its Sanskrit and romanization were imported separately from its translations and commentary. The seven files contain 23,291 addressed records in 648 sargas. These are digital record counts, not a claim that every traditional verse has been correctly transcribed.

The numbered Sanskrit at IIT Kanpur’s Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa project, which presents the Southern Recension, is a comparison source. A parallel numbered Sanskrit text helps readers follow the opening sarga’s verse boundaries. The separate Baroda Critical Edition has different readings and numbering in places.

Reading the translations

English and Hindi translations are independently written for this edition. Published coverage currently runs from Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.1.1 through 1.1.70. The first seventeen retain their provisional editorial status. Verses 1.1.18–1.1.70 have received a verse-boundary check, with corrections where needed. In 1.1.61–1.1.70, four overlays restore boundaries and six normalize displays on already correctly bounded rows; each preserves access to the imported reading. IIT Kanpur evidence for this check was available through indexed Sanskrit extracts; direct access was unavailable. The complete numbered parallel was also consulted. This does not constitute a full critical collation or independent human review.

A Sanskrit sentence sometimes crosses several numbered verses. Connected-verse notes explain these links; an unfinished sentence remains unfinished at its verse boundary. Text is not moved to make each translation stand alone.

Modern context and key teachings are labelled editorial reflections. They are separate from both the Sanskrit and its translation. Verses awaiting translation are marked clearly.
